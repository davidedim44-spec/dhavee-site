Immanuel Kant

Biography

Immanuel Kant (1724–1804) was a German philosopher from Königsberg. He is considered one of the central figures of modern philosophy. Kant sought to reconcile rationalism and empiricism, and his works profoundly influenced ethics, metaphysics, and epistemology.

Main Ideas

  • Categorical Imperative: Moral actions must be guided by universal principles that apply to all rational beings.
  • Critique of Pure Reason: Human knowledge is shaped by both sensory experience and innate categories of understanding.
  • Autonomy and Duty: True morality arises from acting out of duty, not inclination.
  • Limits of Reason: Reason can structure experience but cannot fully grasp things-in-themselves (noumena).

Legacy

Kant’s philosophy reshaped modern thought, influencing German Idealism, existentialism, and contemporary ethics. His ideas on duty and universal moral law remain central to debates in moral philosophy, while his epistemology continues to shape discussions on knowledge and perception.
