Friedrich Nietzsche

Biography

Friedrich Nietzsche (1844–1900) was a German philosopher, cultural critic, and philologist. He challenged traditional morality, religion, and philosophy, advocating for a radical re‑evaluation of values. His writings were often provocative and poetic, influencing existentialism and postmodern thought.

Main Ideas

  • “God is dead”: A declaration of the decline of traditional religious authority in modern society.
  • Will to Power: A fundamental drive shaping human behavior and creativity.
  • Übermensch (Overman): An ideal figure who creates new values beyond conventional morality.
  • Eternal Recurrence: The idea that life repeats infinitely, challenging individuals to affirm existence fully.

Legacy

Nietzsche’s philosophy influenced existentialists like Sartre and Camus, as well as postmodern thinkers such as Foucault and Derrida. His critique of morality and emphasis on self‑creation continue to inspire debates in philosophy, literature, and cultural studies.
