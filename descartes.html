René Descartes

Biography

René Descartes (1596–1650) was a French philosopher, mathematician, and scientist. Often called the “Father of Modern Philosophy,” he sought to establish a foundation for knowledge based on reason and certainty. His work bridged medieval scholasticism and the scientific revolution.

Main Ideas

  • Cogito, ergo sum: “I think, therefore I am” — the indubitable foundation of knowledge.
  • Method of Doubt: Systematic skepticism to strip away uncertain beliefs.
  • Dualism: Separation of mind (res cogitans) and body (res extensa).
  • Mathematical Method: Applied mathematical reasoning to philosophy and science.

Legacy

Descartes’ emphasis on reason and skepticism shaped modern philosophy and science. His dualism influenced debates on consciousness, while his rationalist approach inspired later thinkers such as Spinoza and Leibniz. His ideas remain central to discussions of knowledge, mind, and reality.
