Research Hub

This section explores major branches of philosophy across history and cultures. Each branch includes key thinkers, their main ideas, and their lasting legacies.

Ancient Philosophy

Greek and Roman thinkers who laid the foundations of Western thought.

  • Plato — Theory of Forms, justice, knowledge.
  • Aristotle — Logic, ethics, politics, natural science.
  • Stoics — Virtue, reason, resilience.

Modern Philosophy

17th–18th century thinkers who reshaped metaphysics, epistemology, and ethics.

  • René Descartes — Rationalism and “I think, therefore I am.”
  • Immanuel Kant — Duty, categorical imperative, limits of reason.
  • David Hume — Empiricism, skepticism, causation.
  • Baruch Spinoza — God or Nature, determinism, ethics.

Contemporary Philosophy

19th–20th century thinkers exploring freedom, power, language, and identity.

  • Friedrich Nietzsche — Will to power, Übermensch, eternal recurrence.
  • Jean‑Paul Sartre — Existentialism, radical freedom, bad faith.
  • Michel Foucault — Power, discipline, biopolitics.
  • Jacques Derrida — Deconstruction, différance, critique of metaphysics.

African Philosophy

Philosophical traditions rooted in African identity, community, and liberation.

  • Kwame Nkrumah — Pan‑Africanism, Consciencism.
  • John Mbiti — African religions, community, time.
  • Sophie Oluwole — Yoruba philosophy, Orunmila tradition.
  • Paulin Hountondji — Critique of ethnophilosophy.
  • Placide Tempels — Vital force, Bantu philosophy.
