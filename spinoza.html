Baruch Spinoza

Biography

Baruch Spinoza (1632–1677) was a Dutch philosopher of Sephardic Jewish origin. He lived a modest life in Amsterdam and The Hague, working as a lens grinder while writing philosophical works. His radical ideas on God, nature, and freedom led to his excommunication from the Jewish community, but his writings became central to Enlightenment thought.

Main Ideas

  • God or Nature: Identified God with the natural world, rejecting traditional religious views.
  • Determinism: Believed everything follows from the necessity of divine nature.
  • Ethics: True happiness comes from rational understanding and intellectual love of God.
  • Freedom: Freedom is achieved through knowledge and rational self‑mastery, not free will.

Legacy

Spinoza’s philosophy influenced Enlightenment thinkers, modern secularism, and later figures such as Hegel and Einstein. His rationalist approach and naturalistic view of God continue to shape debates in metaphysics, ethics, and theology.
