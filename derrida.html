Jacques Derrida

Biography

Jacques Derrida (1930–2004) was a French philosopher best known as the founder of deconstruction. He studied at the École Normale Supérieure in Paris and became one of the most influential figures in late 20th‑century philosophy, challenging traditional approaches to language, meaning, and truth.

Main Ideas

  • Deconstruction: A method of analyzing texts to reveal internal contradictions and instability of meaning.
  • Différance: Meaning is always deferred and shaped by differences between words, never fully present.
  • Critique of Metaphysics: Questioned the idea of fixed truths or stable foundations in philosophy.
  • Language and Texts: Emphasized that interpretation is endless, and texts cannot be reduced to a single meaning.

Legacy

Derrida’s work influenced literary theory, cultural studies, law, and philosophy. His ideas reshaped how scholars approach texts, meaning, and interpretation, and continue to spark debates about truth, language, and postmodern thought.
