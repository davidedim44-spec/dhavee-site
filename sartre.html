Jean‑Paul Sartre

Biography

Jean‑Paul Sartre (1905–1980) was a French philosopher, novelist, and playwright. He was a leading figure in existentialism and a prominent public intellectual in mid‑20th century Europe. Sartre emphasized human freedom, responsibility, and the creation of meaning in a world without predetermined values.

Main Ideas

  • Existence precedes essence: Humans are not born with fixed natures; they define themselves through choices.
  • Radical freedom: Individuals are condemned to be free, responsible for their actions and meaning.
  • Bad faith: Self‑deception occurs when people deny their freedom and responsibility.
  • Engagement: Intellectuals and writers must act in the world, not remain detached observers.

Legacy

Sartre’s existentialism influenced philosophy, literature, and political activism. His ideas shaped post‑war debates on freedom and responsibility, and his works continue to inspire discussions on authenticity, ethics, and human existence.
