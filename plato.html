Plato

Plato (427–347 BCE) was a student of Socrates and the founder of the Academy in Athens. His writings shaped Western philosophy for centuries, especially in metaphysics, ethics, and political theory.

Main Ideas

  • The Theory of Forms: Reality consists of eternal, perfect “Forms” beyond the physical world.
  • The Allegory of the Cave: Human beings mistake shadows for reality until they seek true knowledge.
  • Political Philosophy: Advocated for rule by philosopher-kings in his work The Republic.

Legacy

Plato’s works influenced countless thinkers, including Aristotle. His dialogues remain central texts in philosophy, exploring justice, knowledge, and the nature of reality.
