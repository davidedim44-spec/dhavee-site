Plato

Biography

Plato (427–347 BCE) was a student of Socrates and later the teacher of Aristotle. He founded the Academy in Athens, one of the earliest institutions of higher learning. His dialogues remain some of the most influential works in philosophy, covering ethics, politics, metaphysics, and epistemology.

Main Ideas

  • The Theory of Forms: True reality exists beyond the physical world in eternal, perfect “Forms.”
  • The Allegory of the Cave: Humans mistake shadows for reality until they seek true knowledge.
  • Political Philosophy: Advocated for rule by philosopher-kings in The Republic.
  • Epistemology: Knowledge is recollection of eternal truths.

Legacy

Plato’s works shaped Western philosophy and influenced countless thinkers, including Aristotle, Augustine, and Descartes. His ideas on justice, truth, and the nature of reality continue to inspire philosophical inquiry today.
