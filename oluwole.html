Sophie Oluwole

Biography

Sophie Bosede Oluwole (1935–2018) was a Nigerian philosopher and the first woman in Nigeria to earn a doctorate in philosophy. She taught at the University of Lagos and became a leading voice in African philosophy, emphasizing the importance of indigenous Yoruba thought and oral traditions.

Main Ideas

  • Yoruba Philosophy: Advocated for recognition of Yoruba thinkers, especially Orunmila, as philosophers equal to Plato or Socrates.
  • Oral Tradition: Highlighted the philosophical depth of African oral literature, myths, and proverbs.
  • Gender and Philosophy: Championed women’s voices in African intellectual traditions.
  • Comparative Philosophy: Drew parallels between Yoruba thought and classical Western philosophy.

Legacy

Oluwole’s work challenged the dominance of Western philosophy in African academia and validated indigenous knowledge systems. She remains a symbol of intellectual courage, inspiring new generations to explore African traditions as philosophy in their own right.
