Socrates

Socrates (469–399 BCE) is considered the father of Western philosophy. He is best known for his method of questioning — the Socratic Method — which sought to expose contradictions in thought and lead people toward truth and virtue.

Main Ideas

  • “The unexamined life is not worth living.”
  • Virtue is knowledge — to know the good is to do the good.
  • Philosophy as dialogue and critical questioning.

Legacy

Socrates never wrote down his teachings; his ideas survive through the works of his students, especially Plato. His trial and death remain a powerful symbol of intellectual freedom and the pursuit of truth.
