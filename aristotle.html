Aristotle

Biography

Aristotle (384–322 BCE) was a student of Plato and tutor to Alexander the Great. He founded the Lyceum in Athens and developed a comprehensive system of philosophy that covered logic, ethics, politics, metaphysics, and natural sciences. Unlike Plato, Aristotle emphasized empirical observation and practical reasoning.

Main Ideas

  • Logic: Formalized deductive reasoning and introduced the syllogism.
  • Ethics: Advocated the “Golden Mean” — virtue lies between extremes.
  • Politics: Classified governments and emphasized the role of the polis (city-state).
  • Natural Philosophy: Studied biology, physics, and the natural world systematically.

Legacy

Aristotle’s works influenced medieval scholarship and remain foundational in philosophy and science. His emphasis on empirical observation shaped the development of modern scientific methods, while his ethical and political theories continue to inform debates today.
